## Post

```
In my mentoring sessions, and in the community spaces I am part of, one frustration keeps coming up from people new to Toronto: getting to work on time. Trains run late, and they never quite know how early to leave.

So I turned it into a data project. Tayo is an imaginary newcomer, based on what I hear most often: he moved here in August, and his new job starts at 9:00.

I analysed 15,800 real TTC subway delays (January 2025 to August 2026) to see what is actually going on:

• Half of all lost time comes from people-related incidents, not broken trains.
• Bloor-Yonge from 3 to 7 p.m. loses more time to these incidents than any other station and time slot.
• Sunday at 8 a.m. is the riskiest ride of the week, and a 5-minute buffer beats half of all delays.

I also built a simple planner Tayo could use to decide how early to leave.

Built with Python, Excel and Power BI. Full project in the comments.

Which station costs you the most time?
```
