    How it is built. Public comments from 2025 to 2026 on four platforms: YouTube comments under news videos about INEC (Channels Television, Arise News, TVC News, SaharaTV and four commentary channels, through the vidIQ connector), replies on X to INEC and news posts plus keyword searches, comments under INEC Nigeria’s Facebook posts, and Nairaland politics threads. The X search results are shown in the charts but left out of the headline shares, because a search for “rig 2027” finds rigging posts by construction. Author names were never stored. Each comment was tagged into fourteen fear themes with English and Pidgin keyword rules; in a hand check of 50 comments, 45 were tagged fully correctly. Court comments under two videos about pre-election cases (party deadlines and a recall petition) were moved to a separate theme under step 2, so the courts step only counts results disputes. Mapping rigging to collation is an assumption: some rigging comments are about ballot snatching at the unit. Only comments raising a concern are counted, and a few of them dismiss the concern. 2023 polling unit data: the Center for Collaborative Investigative Journalism audit of all 176,846 IReV result sheets. Survey figures: Yiaga Africa (September 2026) and Afrobarometer. Every figure on this page is calculated from the attached files; Sources shows each one.
    Limits. Online commenters are younger, more urban and more online than voters as a whole, and the result depends on which videos, posts and threads were sampled; this measures fear, not voting intention, and it is not a poll. Three videos with openly partisan titles supply about a third of the fear comments. English and Pidgin only. No party or candidate is scored.
    Words used. INEC: Independent National Electoral Commission. PVC: permanent voter card. BVAS: the device that checks voters and photographs the unit result sheet. IReV: INEC’s public results website. EC8A: the polling unit result sheet. Collation: adding up unit results at ward, local government and state centres. CCIJ: Center for Collaborative Investigative Journalism.
