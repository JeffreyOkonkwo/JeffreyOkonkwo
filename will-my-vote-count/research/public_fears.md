# What Nigerians Fear, Distrust and Complain About Ahead of the 2027 General Elections

Research compiled 10 October 2026. Every fact carries a source URL and the date of the source. Items marked **[UNCERTAIN]** have a conflict between sources, a missing method, or a figure that could not be confirmed against a primary document. Secondary press reports were used where the primary document could not be fetched (for example, Yiaga Africa's NVIS site blocks automated fetching).

---

## TL;DR

- **Distrust of INEC is the headline finding.** Only 34% of Nigerians are confident INEC will run credible 2027 elections, down from 45% in December 2025 and March 2026 (Yiaga Africa NVIS round 3, September 2026). Afrobarometer found only 16% trust the electoral commission "somewhat" or "a lot" (Round 10, mid 2024), one of the lowest figures in Africa.
- **Fear of violence is the main barrier to voting.** 61% say violence could stop them voting (78% in the North-East), and 82% are very concerned insecurity will affect the conduct of the polls (Yiaga, September 2026). ACLED says Q2 2026 was Nigeria's most violent quarter since 1997.
- **People intend to vote, but commitment is weak.** 84% say they are likely to vote, but only 41% are certain and committed. Young people are much less likely to vote than older people (Yiaga, September 2026). Turnout in 2023 was about 27%, the lowest since 1999.
- **Vote buying is normal and visible.** 19% say they have received money to vote a certain way (32% in the North-West). Observers reported up to N50,000 per vote and food handouts in the August 2026 Osun election.
- **The new Electoral Act made the main trust issue worse.** The Electoral Act 2026, signed 18 February 2026, made electronic transmission of results optional and kept the manual Form EC8A as the primary record. This led to the #OccupyNASS protests.
- **Fear of a one-party state is rising.** APC holds 31 of 36 governorships (March 2026). INEC delisted the ADC coalition's leadership in April 2026, and the PDP is split by court rulings. The opposition says INEC is partisan.
- **Courts are not trusted to fix disputes.** 63% lack confidence in the judiciary to resolve election disputes fairly (81% in the South-South). "Go to court" is now said sarcastically.
- **There is no public, theme-level social listening baseline for 2027.** The closest are Logically's 2023 study (6.5% of Obi-related posts claimed rigging after the results) and CDD and HumAngle disinformation studies. This is a real gap.

## 1. Survey evidence

### 1.1 Yiaga Africa National Voting Intentions Survey (NVIS), three rounds

| Indicator | Round 1 (1 to 5 Dec 2025) | Round 2 (Mar 2026) | Round 3 (Sep 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Likely or very likely to vote | 77% | 73% | 84% |
| "Very likely" to vote | 52% | 48% | 47% |
| Confident in INEC | 45% | 45% | 34% |
| Fear of violence as a barrier | 42% (main barrier) | n/a | 61% |
| Aware of electoral reforms | 24% | 37% | 62% |

Sources:
- Round 3: Premium Times via allAfrica, 7 to 8 Oct 2026, https://allafrica.com/stories/202610080096.html ; Channels TV, 7 Oct 2026, https://www.channelstv.com/2026/10/07/2027-84-of-nigerians-willing-to-vote-61-fear-violence-yiaga-africa/
- Round 1: allAfrica, 31 Dec 2025, https://allafrica.com/stories/202512310091.html ; Yiaga, Dec 2025, https://yiaga.org/yiaga-africa-releases-first-national-voting-intentions-survey-ahead-of-2027-elections/

**Round 3 detail (fieldwork September 2026, n = 2,470 adults, 36 states plus FCT, 2,066 phone interviews and 404 face to face, mostly rural)** (allAfrica, 8 Oct 2026, link above):
- 41% are "certain and committed" to vote. **[UNCERTAIN]** The report does not make clear whether this is 41% of all respondents or 41% of the 84%.
- 44% of likely voters had not chosen a candidate.
- 61% fear violence could stop them voting. In the North-East the figure is 78%.
- 82% are very concerned that insecurity could affect how the elections are run.
- 48% trust security agencies to protect their votes.
- 83% say better security would motivate them to vote, compared with 38% for better logistics and 20% for stronger candidates.
- 34% are confident in INEC. 62% of South-East respondents lack confidence.
- 49% trust election technology (BVAS, IReV) more than INEC itself.
- 63% lack confidence in the judiciary to resolve disputes fairly. The South-South figure is 81%.
- 19% say they received money to vote for a particular candidate. The figure is 32% in the North-West and 24% among young people.
- Young people: 40% likely to vote, compared with 64% of those aged 61 and over. **[UNCERTAIN]** Channels TV reports the 40% as "very likely". Young people also report more fear of violence, lower trust in INEC and more reliance on social media (Channels, 7 Oct 2026).
- Gender: 65% of men and 57% of women are likely to vote.
- Motivations: 49% want a change of leadership, 20% cite civic duty and 16% cite specific candidates. Channels TV also reports economy and jobs at 38% and security at 36%.
- 71% rate the economy poor or very poor. 54% approve of the government's performance.

**Round 1 detail (December 2025)** (allAfrica, 31 Dec 2025):
- 76% believe their vote can influence outcomes.
- 34% lack confidence in INEC and 45% are confident.
- 76% are unaware of recent reforms. 64% of those who are aware report higher confidence.
- Main voting factors: security 21%, candidate quality 21%, economy 17%.
- Trust in INEC is weaker in the South-East and South-South and stronger in the North.

**[UNCERTAIN]** In Round 1, 34% *lacked* confidence. In Round 3, 34% *had* confidence. These are separate measures, so this is not a contradiction, but anyone quoting the figures should be careful not to swap them.

### 1.2 Afrobarometer (Nigeria)

**Round 10 (fieldwork June to July 2024).** Source: Afrobarometer AD1128, 8 Feb 2026, https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/AD1128-PAP5-Africans-want-elections-but-dont-trust-commissions-to-ensure-theyre-free-and-fair-Afrobarometer-8feb26.pdf
- 16% trust the electoral commission "somewhat" or "a lot". 83% trust it "just a little" or "not at all". This is one of the lowest results among the 38 countries surveyed. The 37-country average is 38%.
- 43% say the last national election (2023) was free and fair. 55% say it was not free and fair, or had major problems. The continental average is 55% free and fair.
- 23% feared political intimidation or violence during the campaign. The continental average is 19%.
- 42% think powerful people could find out how they voted. The continental average is 28%.
- About 74% support choosing leaders through elections. **[UNCERTAIN]** This figure was read from a chart, not stated in the text.

**Round 9 (fieldwork 5 to 31 March 2022).** Source: Afrobarometer AD598, 30 Jan 2023, https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/AD598-Nigerians-want-competitive-elections-but-dont-trust-the-electoral-commission-Afrobarometer-30jan23.pdf
- 23% trust INEC, down 12 points from about 35% in 2017.
- 56% said the 2019 election was free and fair.
- 32% feared violence in 2019 ("a lot" 12%, "somewhat" 20%).

**Trend: trust in INEC fell from about 35% (2017) to 23% (2022) to 16% (2024).**

### 1.3 SBM Intelligence

**Nigeria 2027 Voter Sentiment Tracker, wave 1 (fieldwork third week of May 2026, n = 829, eight states plus FCT, face to face).** Source: Nairametrics, 14 Jun 2026, https://nairametrics.com/2026/06/14/2027-poll-nearly-80-of-nigerians-say-country-moving-in-wrong-direction/
- Nearly 80% say the country is going in the wrong direction. By zone: South-East 92.2%, North-Central 90.1%, South-South 89%, North-West 88.2%, North-East 63.5%, South-West 60.3%.
- Top national concern: insecurity and terrorism 45%, economic hardship and insecurity together 34%, mainly economic issues 13%.
- Tinubu's net favourability is -58.5.
- Intention to turn out: South-East 87.4%, South-South 82.9%, North-West 82.4%.
- **[UNCERTAIN]** The margin of error and weighting are not published.

**"The Algorithm and the Ballot Box" (fieldwork April to May 2026, n = 829).** Sources: Nairametrics, 1 Jul 2026, https://nairametrics.com/2026/07/01/sbm-intelligence-66-5-of-nigerians-fear-ai-could-disrupt-2027-elections/ ; TechCabal, 2 Jul 2026, https://techcabal.com/2026/07/02/nigeria-heads-into-2027-election-worried-about-ai/ ; SBM, https://sbmintelligence.substack.com/p/the-algorithm-and-the-ballot-box
- 66.5% are extremely or very concerned about AI-generated political misinformation.
- 52.1% say social media (WhatsApp, Facebook, X, TikTok) is their main source of political news. WhatsApp is used by about 95.1% of internet users.
- About 12% do not verify political information before sharing it. 53.3% cross-check with other news sources and 20.9% search online.
- The "Southeast paradox": 82.5% of South-East respondents rely on social media and 42.7% do not verify, yet only 38.9% are concerned about AI misinformation.
- On foreign influence, 35.7% name the United States, 11% China and 7.5% the UK. 11.9% simply answered "Dollar".

**Post-2023:** Over 70% doubted the credibility of the 2023 process (SBM poll cited in a BusinessDay editorial, 13 Oct 2025, https://businessday.ng/editorial/article/the-judiciary-and-the-future-of-nigerias-democracy/). **[UNCERTAIN]** The original SBM release was not located.

### 1.4 Citizen by Zikoko and TechCabal Insights, Citizen Report 2026 (youth, n of about 10,681, 2025 fieldwork)

Sources: report PDF, Feb 2026, https://citizen.zikoko.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/02/Citizen-Report-2026-e.pdf ; TechCabal Insights, 6 Mar 2026, https://insights.techcabal.com/?p=1302
- Intention to vote in 2027: 48.5% definitely, 29.2% probably, 17.3% unsure, 5% reluctant.
- More than 70% have only some or very little trust in INEC.
- 21% have witnessed vote buying (men 30%, women 27%). **[UNCERTAIN]** These subgroup figures are both above the overall figure, which suggests the subgroups use a different base.

- 83.3% have been exposed to fake news, deepfakes or manipulated content. 68.2% say it influenced their voting decision, and 39.2% of those were discouraged from voting.
- 78% say economic hardship influences their vote. 62% say exposure to irregularities reduces their motivation.
- Over 80% of respondents are under 35. **[UNCERTAIN]** This is an opt-in sample, not a probability sample.

**Dominant fear themes online in 2023, in rough order of documented volume:** (1) rigging and stolen-mandate claims, peaking after the results; (2) fake results and IReV upload failures ("glitch"); (3) ethnic and religious framing, especially Igbo targeting in Lagos; (4) resistance, revolution and "no be my president" calls; (5) recycled violence and vote-buying footage. **[UNCERTAIN]** This ordering is my synthesis. No study measured all of these themes on the same base.

**Not found:** a Dataphyte or Meltwater social listening theme study for 2023 or 2026, or a published Yiaga #WatchingTheVote social media report. Dataphyte publishes election data and turnout analysis, not sentiment analysis.

## 3. Political context as of October 2026

### 3.1 Timetable and law
- **Presidential and National Assembly elections: Saturday 16 January 2027. Governorship and state assembly elections: 6 February 2027.** The original dates were 20 Feb and 6 Mar 2027. They were moved after the Electoral Act 2026 came into force. Muslim groups had objected that the earlier dates clashed with Ramadan. Source: Guardian, 26 Feb 2026, https://guardian.ng/news/inec-moves-2027-presidential-election-to-january-16-2/
- Presidential campaigns run from 19 Aug 2026 to 15 Jan 2027 (Nairametrics, 1 Jul 2026, link above).
- **Electoral Act 2026 (signed 18 Feb 2026).** Electronic transmission of results is optional and at INEC's discretion. Manual collation on Form EC8A remains the primary legal record and takes precedence over uploads. Tinubu cited broadband coverage and hacking risk. ALTON, the telecom operators' body, cited over 70% 3G and 4G coverage against that argument. Sources: SBM, 20 Feb 2026, https://sbmintelligence.substack.com/p/nigeria-makes-e-results-optional ; Channels, 18 Feb 2026, https://www.channelstv.com/2026/02/18/breaking-tinubu-signs-electoral-bill-into-law
- Former INEC officials, including Festus Okoye, say Sections 60, 65 and 155 conflict and that BVAS and "Smart Card Reader" are used inconsistently in the text (Guardian, 27 Feb 2026, https://guardian.ng/news/ex-inec-officials-fault-2026-electoral-act-demand-clarity-on-bvas-e-transmission/).
- **Certificate forgery was removed as a ground for an election petition** (Section 138). Petitioners now face fines of N5m to N10m for petitions on other grounds. SAN Okutepa called this "an outrage" (BusinessDay, 8 Mar 2026, https://businessday.ng/life/article/electoral-act-outrage-over-removal-of-certificate-forgery-as-ground-for-election-petition/).
- The House passed a ban on dual party membership. Situation Room called it "politically targeted" (allAfrica, 31 Mar 2026, https://allafrica.com/stories/202604010071.html).
- **Protests:** "Occupy the National Assembly" on 9 Feb 2026 was led by the Obidient Movement, and Peter Obi joined. Placards read "Our votes must count" and "No to electoral robbery" (BusinessDay, 10 Feb 2026, https://businessday.ng/news/article/protests-erupt-as-nigerians-demand-real-time-election-results/). Sowore alleged that police used an "unfamiliar gas" (News Central, https://newscentraltv.com/?p=385747).

### 3.2 Candidates and coalitions
- **APC:** Tinubu, with Shettima confirmed as running mate. The Muslim-Muslim ticket stands again (ThisDay, 11 Jul 2026, https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/11/2027-apc-confirms-shettima-as-tinubus-running-mate/). APC has **31 of 36 governors** after Zamfara's Lawal defected (BusinessDay, 9 Mar 2026, https://businessday.ng/politics/article/pdp-depletes-apc-governors-rise-to-31-as-lawal-joins-fold/).
- **ADC coalition:** Atiku won the primary with 1,846,370 votes, against Amaechi's 504,117 and Hayatu-Deen's 177,120 (NAN, 27 May 2026, https://nannews.ng/ticket-6/). Amaechi is the running mate (ICIR, https://www.icirnigeria.org/adc-names-amaechi-as-atikus-running-mate/). INEC delisted the David Mark and Aregbesola leadership on 1 Apr 2026, citing a court dispute (Arise, https://www.arise.tv/adc-crisis-deepens-as-inec-delists-mark-aregbesola-suspends-recognition/). Atiku and Obi then led a protest at INEC on 8 Apr 2026 against the "enthronement of a dictatorship" and a "one-party system" (Sahara Reporters, https://saharareporters.com/2026/04/08/we-will-stop-enthronement-dictatorship-atiku-says-joins-adc-mass-protest-abuja-against).
- **NDC:** Peter Obi left Labour for ADC around January 2026, then left ADC, calling it "toxic". He joined NDC with Kwankwaso on 3 May 2026 (ICIR, https://www.icirnigeria.org/2027-obi-kwankwaso-dump-adc-join-ndc/). Obi is the candidate and Kwankwaso the running mate (Gazette, July 2026, https://gazettengr.com/peter-obi-emerges-ndc-presidential-candidate-selects-kwankwaso-as-running-mate/).
- **PDP:** The party is split between a Wike-backed faction, which INEC recognised on 30 Mar 2026, and a Turaki-led faction. The Court of Appeal ruled against Turaki on 9 Mar 2026 (ICIR, 30 Mar 2026, https://www.icirnigeria.org/national-convention-inec-recognises-wike-backed-pdp-leadership-amid-deepening-factional-crisis/). Police sealed the venue for a Jonathan endorsement event (Blueprint, https://blueprint.ng/happening-now-pdp-crises-deepen-as-police-seal-venue-for-jonathans-endorsement/). **[UNCERTAIN]** Wikipedia lists Sandy Onor as the PDP nominee. This was not verified in the press.
- **Labour Party:** The Abure faction withdrew Obi's automatic ticket and adopted its own candidate (Leadership, https://leadership.ng/labour-party-abure-faction-adopts-ahanotu-as-presidential-candidate/).
- **Others:** Seyi Makinde (APM). INEC lists 18 presidential candidates in total. Source: Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Nigerian_general_election. **[UNCERTAIN]** Wikipedia is a secondary source.
- Samson Itodo (Yiaga) warns of "elimination of electoral competition" and a "gradual descent towards a one-party state" (International IDEA, 9 Feb 2026, https://www.idea.int/news/democracy-under-pressure-threats-nigerias-2027-elections).

### 3.3 INEC controversies
- Chairman Prof. Joash Amupitan SAN was confirmed in October 2025. An X account in his name allegedly posted "Victory is sure" during the 2023 period. INEC denies he runs it and cites an unnamed forensic review. He was asked at screening whether he was on Tinubu's 2023 tribunal legal team, and he denied it (PLAC, 11 May 2026, https://placng.org/Legist/inec-and-the-burden-of-public-trust-ahead-of-2027/).
- The Movement for Credible Elections, Pat Utomi's group, asked him to step aside (Guardian, 19 Apr 2026, https://guardian.ng/news/mce-demands-inec-chairman-step-aside-over-alleged-credibility-concerns/).
- Voter revalidation, planned for 13 Apr to 29 May 2026, was postponed until after 2027. The register is therefore not cleaned of the dead and duplicates (Guardian, 11 Apr 2026, https://guardian.ng/news/inec-postpones-voter-revalidation-exercise-till-after-2027-polls/).
- The register now holds over 103m voters, with about 10.6m new registrants. Continuous registration closed on 26 Jul 2026, and PVC collection began on 9 Oct 2026. Some PVCs were stolen in Osun. INEC warned about a fake registration portal (Nairametrics, 5 Oct 2026, https://nairametrics.com/2026/10/05/nigerias-voter-register-surpasses-103-million-ahead-of-2027-elections-inec/). **[UNCERTAIN]** The phase totals in that report do not add up (10.77m against 10.60m).
- Downloadable PVCs, for lost or damaged cards only, start from Osun 2026. Ekiti PVC collection was 97.1% (The Nigeria Lawyer, 1 Jul 2026, https://thenigerialawyer.com/downloadable-voter-cards-coming-inec-to-introduce-digital-option-from-osun-2026-2027-polls-as-ekiti-pvc-collection-hits-97-1/).
- INEC says it needs N873.78bn for 2027 (Nairametrics, 1 Jul 2026).
- ThisDay's list of risks includes youth corps members as ad hoc staff, delays by road transport unions, BVAS and IReV dependence on mobile networks, a possible ASUU strike affecting returning officers, and doubts about police neutrality (ThisDay via allAfrica, 1 Jun 2026, https://allafrica.com/stories/202606020371.html).

### 3.4 Recent off-cycle elections (the latest test cases)
- **Anambra (8 Nov 2025):** Soludo was re-elected. Observers reported low turnout and vote buying, and the EFCC arrested suspected vote buyers. Yiaga's PVT validated the result (allAfrica, https://allafrica.com/stories/202511090027.html ; https://allafrica.com/stories/202511180098.html).
- **Ekiti (20 Jun 2026):** Oyebanji (APC) was re-elected with 319,224 votes. Turnout was **36.07%**, the lowest for a regular cycle. Inducements ranged from bread and pounded yam to N5,000 to N20,000 in cash (Dataphyte, https://dataphyte.com/topic/elections/ekiti-governorship-election-2026-historic-re-election-and-record-of-low-voter-participation).
- **Osun (15 Aug 2026):** Accord won 51.9% against APC's 45.2%, with 43% turnout. Yiaga's PVT matched the official results. BVAS malfunctioned in 20% of polling units, and IReV uploads stalled from 8:48pm to about 11:50pm (Arise, 16 Aug 2026, https://www.arise.tv/yiaga-africa-osun-results-match-polling-unit-votes-but-vote-buying-raises-concerns/). Situation Room reported up to **N50,000 per vote**, jollof rice and drinks handed out at polling units, and agents taking names and phone numbers after people voted. Facial authentication failed for elderly voters (Guardian, 17 Aug 2026, https://guardian.ng/?p=2900347).

### 3.5 Insecurity
- ACLED says **Q2 2026 was the most violent quarter since 1997**. Targeting of civilians is up 30%. SBM says kidnap victims are up 66% year on year and ransom paid has tripled to $5.8m (Sahara Reporters, 2 Sep 2026, https://saharareporters.com/2026/09/02/nigerias-most-violent-quarter-1997-tests-tinubus-2027-re-election-bid-report).
- 5,272 people were killed from January to May 2026, according to media tallies (Vanguard via allAfrica, 15 Jun 2026, https://allafrica.com/stories/202606160329.html). **[UNCERTAIN]** The underlying sources for this tally are not named. Amupitan describes a "triad" of social media volatility, AI disinformation and foreign information manipulation, alongside insurgency.
- Campaign thuggery since August 2026: the NDC office in Emohua, Rivers was attacked on 2 Sep 2026 (Legit, https://www.legit.ng/politics/1729314-tension-political-thugs-attack-ndc-office-destroy-billboards/). A PDP meeting in Agbani, Enugu was attacked on 12 Sep 2026, after earlier attacks on 16 and 29 Aug (Sahara Reporters, https://saharareporters.com/2026/09/12/thugs-attack-pdp-meeting-enugu-vandalise-properties-worth-millions-naira). An Accord gathering in Kuje, FCT was also disrupted (https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/armed-thugs-disrupt-accord-party-kuje-attack).

### 3.6 Economy
- Headline inflation was 15.39% in August 2026 and food inflation 19.57% (VON, Sep 2026, https://von.gov.ng/nigerias-headline-inflation-eases-to-15-39-in-august-nbs/ ; Nairametrics, 15 Sep 2026, https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/15/nigerias-food-inflation-drops-for-first-time-in-six-months-to-19-57/). Inflation was nearly 30% in August 2025 (BusinessDay, 13 Oct 2025). People still feel the hardship: 71% rate the economy poor (Yiaga, September 2026).

### 3.7 Ethnic and religious tension
- The US redesignated Nigeria a "Country of Particular Concern" (late 2025), alongside a presidential threat of military action. Christian groups welcomed it. Many Nigerians feared it would inflame divisions and threaten sovereignty (CSIS, 20 Nov 2025, https://www.csis.org/analysis/president-trumps-redesignation-nigeria-country-particular-concern-cpc-serious-well-founded).
- The Muslim-Muslim APC ticket remains a point of contention (Leadership, https://leadership.ng/amid-tension-over-muslim-muslim-ticket-apc-shifts-unveiling-of-shettima-as-tinubus-running-mate/).
- In 2023, Igbo voters were targeted in Lagos with "IPOB" framing (HumAngle, above).

### 3.8 Diaspora and "japa"
- Roughly 17m Nigerians live abroad (NiDCOM) and sent home $21.8bn in remittances in 2025. Diaspora voting is not provided for: the 2022 constitutional amendment failed in the House by 87 to 269, and NIDCOM says it is too late for 2027 (BusinessDay, 11 Jun 2026, https://businessday.ng/investigation/article/diaspora-nigerians-shut-out-of-voting-despite-yearly-21bn-remittances/). **[UNCERTAIN]** The 17m figure is an advocacy estimate.
- About 52,000 Nigerians moved to the UK in 2024: 27,000 on work visas and 22,000 on study visas (ONS via ICIR, 23 May 2025, https://www.icirnigeria.org/japa-over-50000-nigerians-migrated-to-uk-in-2024/). Emigrants stay on the register but cannot vote, which inflates the register and depresses turnout. **[UNCERTAIN]** This last point is an inference. No source quantifies it.

### 3.9 Judiciary
- Every presidential election since 1999 has gone to court. "Go to court" is now used as a sarcastic jab. Judgments are seen as turning on technicalities (BusinessDay editorial, 13 Oct 2025, https://businessday.ng/editorial/article/the-judiciary-and-the-future-of-nigerias-democracy/).
- 63% lack confidence in the courts to settle disputes (Yiaga, September 2026).
- Courts are also deciding party leadership (PDP, ADC, Labour), so the "court will decide" theme now covers the pre-election period too.

## 4. Ranked fear and complaint themes with tagging keywords

**How the ranking was made:** themes are ordered by (a) the share of people affected or concerned in national surveys, (b) prominence in 2025 to 2026 news, and (c) volume in 2023 online studies. **[UNCERTAIN]** The ranking is my judgement and has not been measured on one base. It should be re-weighted once real social data is tagged.

**Keyword notes:** match case-insensitively. Allow common misspellings (for example "inec" and "Inec") and hashtags. Pidgin and slang items marked (P) were compiled from common Nigerian online usage. ### Main gaps (the opportunity)
1. **No public, real-time, multilingual (Pidgin, Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo) tracker of public fears.** Surveys arrive every few months and social studies come after the event.
2. **WhatsApp is a blind spot.** It is the main news channel for most people and is encrypted.
3. **Vote buying is the threat that verification tools miss.** PVT and IReV validate counts, not choices.
4. **Trust in technology (49%) is higher than trust in INEC (34%).** Tools that let citizens check results themselves, such as the IReV polling unit result against a crowd-sourced photo of the posted result, can use that trust.
5. **Young people have low registration, low intention to vote and high reliance on social media.** Mobile-first, Pidgin-first tools are scarce.
6. **Diaspora citizens are fully excluded from voting** but are highly engaged online. They are a ready audience for monitoring and amplification roles.
