## 1. The 2027 election timetable

### 1.1 Official dates (current, revised timetable)

The original timetable set the Presidential and National Assembly elections for 20 February 2027 and the Governorship and State Assembly elections for 6 March 2027. INEC revised it on 26 February 2026, after the Electoral Act 2026 came into force. [Source: https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/top-news/859957-updated-inec-reschedules-2027-general-election-releases-new-election-timetable.html, 26 Feb 2026]

## 2. End-to-end process as a value stream

The stream below runs from voter registration to final court judgment. For each step I give the trigger, the main actors, the inputs and outputs, and the known volumes. Step numbers are reused in the pain point table at the end.

### Step 1. Continuous Voter Registration (CVR)

* Legal basis: the Electoral Act 2026 governs the register; the Commission keeps it in electronic form in a central database. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ElectoralAct2026.pdf, fetched; also published at https://inecnigeria.org/documents/Electoral-Act-2026.pdf, fetched, identical file size]
* Phases for the 2027 cycle [Source: https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/headlines/914925-2027-middle-aged-voters-dominate-nigerias-103m-voter-register-inec.html, 6 Oct 2026; and Channels TV, 29 Sep 2026]:

| Phase | Dates | New registrations |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 Aug 2025 to 10 Dec 2025 | 2,611,266 |
| 2 | 5 Jan 2026 to 17 Apr 2026 | 4,121,835 (Channels) or 4,121,825 (Premium Times) |
| 3 | 11 May 2026 to 26 Jul 2026 | 3,867,997 |
| Sum of phases | | 10,601,098 (My calculation) |
| Headline total given by INEC | | 10,772,421 |

INEC has not explained it in any source I found. Possible explanations (not confirmed): online pre-registrations, transfers, or a fourth window. [Sources: https://www.channelstv.com/2026/09/29/2027-inec-fixes-october-9-for-pvc-collection-records-10-7m-registered-voters-1058758/, 29 Sep 2026; https://nairametrics.com/2026/10/05/nigerias-voter-register-surpasses-103-million-ahead-of-2027-elections-inec/, 5 Oct 2026]

* Separate claim: "9.3m youths registered as new voters in 2026". [Source: https://tribuneonlineng.com/9-3m-youths-registered-as-new-voters-in-2026-inec/, not fetched] UNCERTAIN: I could not reconcile this with the phase totals.

### Step 2. PVC printing, distribution and collection

* Legal rule: a voter must present a Permanent Voter Card (PVC) for accreditation. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 47(1), fetched]
* New in 2026: Section 18(1) says nothing prevents a card owner "from printing a downloadable copy". Replacement PVCs may not be issued less than 90 days before polling day (Section 18(2)). [Source: Electoral Act 2026, fetched] INEC said it has "introduced printable PVCs under Section 18(1)". [Source: Nairametrics, 5 Oct 2026] UNCERTAIN: I found no INEC guidance yet on whether a printed downloadable copy will be accepted at the polling unit in place of the plastic card.
* Phase 1 PVCs were processed and delivered to all LGA offices before 5 October 2026. Nationwide collection began 9 October 2026. [Source: Nairametrics, 5 Oct 2026; Channels TV, 29 Sep 2026] INEC says collection is in person, not by proxy. [Source: https://www.interceptng.com/politics/inec-says-pvcs-wont-be-collected-by-proxy/, not fetched; date unknown]
* End date of the collection window for 2027: not found. UNCERTAIN.
* Buying and selling PVCs or votes: fine of not less than N5 million or not less than two years' imprisonment or both, plus at least 10 years' ban from contesting. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 22, fetched]

**2023 collection figures**

* 87,209,007 PVCs collected out of 93,469,008 registered (93.3 percent); 6,259,229 uncollected (6.7 percent). Lagos had the most collected (6,214,970) and the most uncollected (845,225), followed by Oyo (515,254 uncollected) and Ogun (410,281). Announced by INEC Chairman Mahmood Yakubu. [Source: https://kolaking.substack.com/p/inec-87m-pvcs-collected-lagos-leads, 23 Feb 2023, reposting TheCable]
* Collection ran from 12 December 2022 to 5 February 2023 after INEC extended the deadline twice. INEC did not disclose how many of the 9.5 million new 2023 registrants collected their cards, and did not break data down to polling unit level until near the end of the process. Observers saw poor logistics, wrong collection offices, long queues, proxy collection and PVC buying. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]
* State by state collection rates for 2023: INEC published a file at https://inecnigeria.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/PVCs-Collected-Registered-Voters.pdf. It now returns HTTP 404 after INEC rebuilt its website. Not fetched. The Wayback Machine copy could not be reached from my environment.
* Older backlog: INEC said in its 2023 election review that over six million PVCs were uncollected, many dating back to 2015, and floated withdrawing cards uncollected for over a decade. [Source: https://tribuneonlineng.com/why-we-might-destroy-over-six-million-uncollected-pvcs-inec/, 27 Dec 2024] INEC later said it never planned to destroy them. [Source: https://allafrica.com/stories/202501220291.html, Jan 2025, citing INEC Facebook post of 5 Jan 2025]
* Current national uncollected count for 2027: not published in any source I found. UNCERTAIN. Data gap.

### Step 3. Polling unit network

* 176,846 polling units. These were created in June 2021 when INEC converted 56,872 voting points and voting point settlements into full polling units, up from 119,974 units set in 1996. Thresholds of 500 and 750 voters were used. [Source: https://www.channelstv.com/2021/06/16/inec-unveils-56872-more-polling-units-across-nigeria, 16 Jun 2021]
* In 2023, 240 polling units in 28 states had no registered voters, so no voting took place in them, leaving 176,606 active units. [Source: https://ripplesnigeria.com/see-full-list-of-240-polling-units-where-elections-will-not-hold, 13 Feb 2023]
* For 2027 INEC cites 176,846 polling units. [Source: NDR, 6 Oct 2026]
* Large units: where a polling unit has more than 1,250 voters, more than one BVAS may be deployed. INEC must publish the list of such units at least 14 days before election day. [Source: INEC Regulations and Guidelines 2026, OCR, fetched]
* In the FCT Area Council elections of 21 February 2026, units with over 1,250 voters were split into nearby units on the same premises. Some voters could not find their units. [Source: https://situationroomng.org/inec-press-release-after-the-2026-fct-area-council-elections-22nd-february-2026/, 25 Feb 2026]
* Voter lookup: https://cvr.inecnigeria.org/pu and https://cvr.inecnigeria.org/locator are linked from the INEC homepage. Both returned HTTP 403 from my environment, so not fetched.

### Step 4. Logistics and materials distribution

* Sensitive materials (ballot papers, result sheets) are held in Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) vaults and moved to LGAs 72 hours before elections, then to Registration Area Centres (RACs). [Source: NDR, 6 Oct 2026]
* Observed handover sequence in 2023: CBN to the Resident Electoral Commissioner in the presence of police, party agents, security and observers; then to LGAs; then to wards; then to RACs on the Friday; then to polling units on election morning. Lagos alone had 20 LGAs, 245 wards and 13,325 polling units. [Source: https://www.icirnigeria.org/inec-commences-distribution-of-election-materials-in-lagos-oyo/, 15 Mar 2023]
* Transport for 2027: renewed agreements with NURTW, RTEAN and MWUN for over 100,000 vehicles and 4,000 boats; special equipment for the Niger Delta, Lake Chad Basin and North East and North West highlands. Coordination across 23 headquarters departments, 36 state offices, the FCT office and 774 LGA offices. [Source: NDR, 6 Oct 2026]
* The Act lets INEC request the armed forces only to secure the distribution and delivery of materials and protect election officials. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 27, fetched]
* RATECH (Registration Area Technical Support) teams sit at RA level. Their job is to fix or replace a failed BVAS, and to confirm at the ward collation centre whether a polling unit result failed to transmit. [Sources: INEC Regulations and Guidelines 2026, OCR, fetched; https://www.naijanews.com/2026/10/06/inec-bvas-2027-election/, 6 Oct 2026; RATECH job advert for 2023 at https://www.myjobmag.com/job/registration-area-technical-support-ratech-inec-1, posted 14 Sep 2022]

### Step 5. Ad hoc staff recruitment and training

* 2027 need: 707,384 ad hoc staff per election day, 1,414,768 engagements over both days, for 176,846 polling units. That is exactly 4 staff per polling unit (my calculation). Most will be NYSC corps members. Recruitment portal open 18 September to 18 November 2026. [Source: NDR, 6 Oct 2026; https://www.channelstv.com/2026/05/11/inec-to-mobilise-over-1-4m-corps-members-for-2027-elections/, 11 May 2026]
* In many states corps members made up nearly 90 percent of presiding officers and registration area officers. [Source: Channels TV, 11 May 2026]
* 2023 baseline: about 1.2 million ad hoc staff, over 70 percent (nearly 850,000) corps members and student volunteers. [Source: Channels TV, 11 May 2026]
* UNCERTAIN: an earlier report quoted the INEC chairman as saying about 450,000 ad hoc staff would be trained, with four poll officers per polling unit. [Source: https://thefrontierng.com/breaking-inec-to-conduct-mock-presidential-election-over-e-transmission-of-results/, undated, written before 26 Feb 2026 because it uses the old election dates] This conflicts with 707,384 per day.
* INEC promised better insurance, welfare and security for corps members. [Source: Channels TV, 11 May 2026]
* Training quality was a weak point in 2023: the EU EOM found that only 31 percent of sampled presidential result forms on IReV were formally and mathematically correct, which it attributed to "serious deficiencies in the training of polling staff"; staff had difficulty completing result forms in 16 of 37 observed units. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]

### Step 6. Accreditation with BVAS

* Legal basis: the Presiding Officer must use the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) or another prescribed device to verify voters. If the BVAS fails and no replacement is deployed, the election in that unit is cancelled and rescheduled within 24 hours if the result would substantially affect the outcome. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 47(2) and (3), fetched]
* Operational rule: on sustained BVAS failure, the Presiding Officer informs the Supervisory Presiding Officer, RATECH, RA Supervisor, the Electoral Operations Support Centre, the LGA Supervisor and the Electoral Officer, and suspends accreditation. If no replacement arrives by 2:30 pm, voting in that unit continues the next day. Voters whose biometric check fails are marked "FA" (Failed Accreditation) on the register. [Source: INEC Regulations and Guidelines 2026, OCR, fetched]
* 2027 technology: 57,000 new BVAS units; software hardening, battery optimisation and biometric calibration of existing units; nationwide inventory and functionality test done. [Source: NDR, 6 Oct 2026; Authority News, 5 Oct 2026]
* 2023 performance (Yiaga Africa sample of 1,507 polling units): BVAS used throughout the day at 99 percent of units; worked properly at 89 percent; malfunctioned and was fixed at 8 percent; malfunctioned and was replaced at 2 percent. [Source: Yiaga Africa post-election statement, 1 Mar 2023, https://wilanglobal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Yiaga-Africa-Post-Election-Statement-on-2023-Presidential-Election.pdf, fetched] (Yiaga's earlier preliminary statement of 26 Feb 2023 said 88 percent and 9 percent: https://yiaga.org/yiaga-africa-watching-the-vote-pvt-2023-presidential-election-preliminary-press-statement/)
* Osun 2026: BVAS worked properly at 80 percent of sampled units and malfunctioned at 20 percent; at 11 percent of units, voters with verified PVCs were denied accreditation because fingerprint or face authentication failed. [Source: https://allafrica.com/stories/202608170131.html, 17 Aug 2026, reporting Yiaga Africa briefing of 16 Aug 2026] Situation Room also reported fingerprint failures, poor face recognition for elderly voters, and long queues where one BVAS served a large unit. [Source: https://situationroomng.org/?p=112001, 17 Aug 2026]

### Step 7. Voting and counting at the polling unit

* Poll opens 8:30 am, closes 2:30 pm (queue rule above). Open secret ballot. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 50, fetched; INEC calendar page, fetched]
* After counting, the Presiding Officer enters scores on Form EC8A, signs and stamps it, has agents countersign, and announces the result at the unit. One recount may be demanded. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Sections 60(1), 60(2), 60(4) and 61, fetched]
* The Presiding Officer must paste the result poster (Form EC60E) at the unit; failure may be prosecuted. [Source: INEC Regulations and Guidelines 2026, OCR, fetched]
* Cancellations at a unit are recorded on Form EC40G and transmitted to IReV. [Same source]

### Step 8. Result upload to IReV (INEC Result Viewing Portal)

* The law now says: "The Presiding Officer shall electronically transmit the results from each polling unit to IREV portal" after Form EC8A is signed, stamped and countersigned; but if transmission fails "as a result of communication failure", the signed Form EC8A "shall at all times remain the primary source of collation and declaration of the result". Wilful breach of Section 60 carries a fine of not less than N500,000 or not less than six months in prison or both. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 60(3) and 60(6), fetched]
* How the press framed it: the Senate voted 55 to 15 to keep transmission effectively optional; critics called the outcome "optional" e-transmission; the President said Nigeria may lack broadband for real-time transmission. [Source: Premium Times, 18 Feb 2026, above; https://sbmintelligence.substack.com/p/nigeria-makes-e-results-optional, 20 Feb 2026] PLAC's reading is more precise: e-transmission is now named in the principal Act, but paper still wins when transmission fails, and the Act attaches no consequence to non-transmission. [Source: https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Factsheet-2-Electronic-Transmission-of-Result-FULL.pdf, Feb 2026, fetched]
* UNCERTAIN: PLAC's factsheet dates the Senate's emergency session as "10 February 2025". From context (Senate first vote on 4 February 2026) this is almost certainly a typo for 10 February 2026.
* Procedure under the 2026 Regulations: the BVAS transmits the validated result (through the e-EC8A) and a scanned image of the EC8A. If upload fails, the Presiding Officer tells everyone present, notifies the SPO and RATECH, records the failure in a report, and delivers the BVAS and original EC8A to the RA/Ward Collation Officer under escort. RATECH and the Ward Collation Officer then confirm whether transmission really failed. If it cannot be fixed, the signed EC8A becomes the primary source. [Source: INEC Regulations and Guidelines 2026, OCR, fetched]
* 2027 upgrades: an enhanced eEC8A system to authenticate result sheets before upload, and a new Artificial Intelligence Division in the ICT Department for result verification, anomaly detection and cyber-intrusion detection. [Source: NDR, 6 Oct 2026]
* The IReV front end is https://www.inecelectionresults.ng (fetched, loads). Its JavaScript points to an API at https://lv001-r.inecelectionresults.ng/api/v1/ and to Amazon S3 buckets `ecollation-result-docs` and `etransmission-result-docs` in the eu-west-2 region. The API host refused connection from my environment (proxy 502), so I could not test bulk data access. [Fetched 10 Oct 2026]

### Step 9. Collation (ward, LGA, state, national)

* Levels in law: Ward (Registration Area) Collation Officer; LGA or Area Council Collation Officer; State Collation Officer; and for the presidency, the National Collation Centre. Returning officers: Ward for councillors, Area Council for chairmen, State constituency, Federal constituency, Senatorial district, State (governor), and National (president). [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 25, fetched]
* A collation officer may only collate after confirming that accredited voters and votes on the form match the figures recorded by BVAS and transmitted directly from polling units. Disputes are settled using the original form, the BVAS data, and the transmitted accreditation and result data. Knowingly collating or announcing a false result is an offence. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 62(4) to (9), fetched]
* If the hard copy from a lower level is missing, the collation officer uses the transmitted result, IReV or the BVAS; failing that, the duplicate copies held by INEC operations, the police and party agents. If none exist, the election in that unit is cancelled. [Source: INEC Regulations and Guidelines 2026, OCR, fetched]
* Collation and returning officers may not make or receive phone calls during collation. [Same source]
* INEC must keep a National Electronic Register of Election Results with polling unit and collated results, and sell certified true copies. [Source: Electoral Act 2026, Section 62(2) and (3), fetched]

### Step 10. Declaration and the margin of lead rule

* Presidential winner must have the most votes and at least 25 percent in at least 24 of 36 states plus the FCT; otherwise a run-off. [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Nigerian_general_election, fetched 10 Oct 2026; reflects the Constitution]
* Where results from some units are cancelled, the Regulations apply the "margin of lead" principle: compare the lead between the top two candidates with the number of collected PVCs (2023 practice) in affected units, and call a supplementary election if the lead is smaller. [Source: INEC Regulations and Guidelines 2026, table of contents and Form EC40G(III) instructions, OCR, fetched] The EU EOM criticised this in 2023 because collected PVC data was not published at unit level in time and the rule leaves room for subjective decisions. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]
* Only INEC may declare results; INEC asked media to set up fact-checking desks. [Source: NDR, 6 Oct 2026]

### Step 11. Petitions and tribunals

* For general elections, the Constitution (Section 285) sets the clock: a petition must be filed within 21 days of the declaration, the tribunal must decide within 180 days, and an appeal must be decided within 60 days. UNCERTAIN: I did not fetch the Constitution text in this session; these are the widely known Section 285 limits. The Electoral Act 2026 itself repeats a 21 day filing rule and a 14 day appeal window for FCT Area Council election petitions (Sections 135 and 136). [Source: Electoral Act 2026, fetched] A pending constitutional bill would cut trial-level time from 180 to 60 days and give the Supreme Court original jurisdiction over presidential petitions. [Source: https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/BILL-SUMMARIES-CONSTITUTION-AMENDMENT-BILLS.pdf, 16 Apr 2026, fetched]
* Courts may not stop primaries or elections while a case is pending. [Source: https://www.thecable.ng/key-provisions-in-the-new-electoral-act-that-you-may-not-know/, 5 Mar 2026, citing Section 88(4)]
* 2023 volume: 1,196 petitions filed; 712 dismissed and 179 withdrawn (891, or 74.5 percent). Presidential 5; Governorship 82; Senate 146; House of Representatives 413; State Assemblies 550. Figures as at 16 October 2023. [Source: https://blueprint.ng/?p=559517, 20 Oct 2023, quoting INEC Commissioner Sam Olumekun]

## 3. What went wrong in 2023 (with numbers)

### 3.1 Headline results and turnout

* Registered 93,469,008; accredited 25,286,616; votes cast 24,965,218; valid 24,025,940; rejected 939,278. Tinubu (APC) 8,794,726; Atiku (PDP) 6,984,520; Obi (LP) 6,101,533; Kwankwaso (NNPP) 1,496,687. Declared at 4:10 am on 1 March 2023. [Source: https://www.thisdaylive.com/2023/03/01/inec-declares-bola-ahmed-tinubu-president-elect/, 1 Mar 2023]
* Turnout: 26.72 percent per the EU EOM, which cites 25,296,616 voters. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched] UNCERTAIN: the EU figure differs by exactly 10,000 from INEC's accredited count of 25,286,616, likely a typo. On INEC figures: 27.05 percent accredited, 26.71 percent votes cast. My calculation. Turnout was 34.75 percent in 2019 and 69.08 percent in 2003. [Source: EU EOM final report, fetched]
* Yiaga's statistical estimate of turnout was 29.4 percent plus or minus 1.0 of registered voters. Yiaga's vote share estimates matched INEC nationally, but INEC's figures for Rivers and Imo were outside Yiaga's ranges (for example, Rivers: INEC APC 44.2 percent against Yiaga 21.7 percent plus or minus 5.0). [Source: Yiaga post-election statement, 1 Mar 2023, fetched]

### 3.2 IReV presidential upload failure

* INEC's explanation, in its report on the 2023 general election released 23 February 2024: at about 4:00 pm on election day, uploads of presidential result sheets returned an HTTP server error, while Senate and House sheets uploaded normally. The system created 470 election types (1 presidential, 109 senatorial, 360 federal constituency) and mapped each to a state; the presidential election belonged to no single state, so the application could not build the folder structure for presidential images. INEC deployed hotfixes; the first presidential sheet uploaded at 8:55 pm. A queue backlog and switched-off or offline BVAS units delayed some uploads to the next day. INEC said this did not affect the credibility of the result. [Source: https://www.channelstv.com/2024/02/23/why-2023-presidential-election-results-failed-to-upload-on-irev-inec/, 23 Feb 2024]
* EU EOM: first results only after 10 pm; 20 percent of results by noon on 26 February; more than a quarter missing at declaration; more than 5 percent still missing at end of March with no explanation. Forms were often uploaded from collation centres rather than polling units, sometimes multiple forms from one BVAS; replacement uploads overwrote originals. In an EU sample of 385 presidential forms on 9 March: 91 percent available, 71 percent readable, 53 percent unaltered, 31 percent formally and mathematically correct. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]
* Yiaga: only 73 percent of presidential polling unit results uploaded by 1 March 2023. In 83 percent of sampled units the Presiding Officer used BVAS to scan the result; in 69 percent he or she attempted transmission. [Source: Yiaga post-election statement, 1 Mar 2023, fetched]
* CCIJ's later full scrape (169,353 files, July 2023 plus earlier caches) found: 14,252 illegible documents (about 8 percent), 12,054 of them flagged by OCR and 10,745 at 192 by 256 pixels; 2,294 sheets for the wrong election; 745 collation sheets uploaded as polling unit results; 22,681 sheets missing at least one of stamp, signature, printed name or agent signature; 1,462 EC40G cancellation forms and 1,217 handwritten cancellations; about 13 million registered voters at units with missing or unusable data. [Source: https://veza.news/article/2025/03/31/broken-promises-of-transparency-a-deep-dive-into-nigerias-2023-election-data/, 31 Mar 2025] I verified status counts in CCIJ's data file (`AllPollingUnitsInfo.csv`, 177,281 rows): 149,205 clear; 12,054 blurred; 7,493 not uploaded; 2,294 wrong election; 1,462 EC40G; 1,217 blank cancellation; 745 collation paper. [Source: https://github.com/anndawn/nigeria-2023-election, cloned 10 Oct 2026]

### 3.3 Late opening of polling units

* Yiaga (presidential, 25 Feb 2023): INEC officials had arrived by 7:30 am in only 27 percent of sampled units. By 9:30 am only 44 percent had begun accreditation and voting nationally: North Central 46, North East 46, North West 44, South West 63, South South 32, South East 11 percent. [Source: Yiaga post-election statement, 1 Mar 2023, fetched]
* EU EOM: polling units opened late in 44 of 50 observed units; poll workers missing in 18; voting started up to five hours late in parts of the South South and South East; opening rated bad or very bad in 21 of 50. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]
* Situation Room 2023 percentage: not found in a fetchable source. The figures often quoted from Situation Room (27 percent open by 10 am in the South East) are from the 2019 election, not 2023. [Source: https://www.thecable.ng/?p=334767, 24 Feb 2019] UNCERTAIN; do not reuse those for 2023.

### 3.4 Collation failures

* EU EOM: chaotic, overcrowded collation centres on election night; many ward collation centres did not open, especially in the South South and South East where presiding officers were sent straight to LGA centres; IReV was not regularly used to verify results at collation. In the 18 March polls, 13 of 41 observed collation centres had discrepancies, and verification against IReV was not done where technical staff were absent (for example in Rivers). [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]

### 3.5 Vote buying

* Yiaga: likely voter inducement at 6 percent of sampled units (7 percent in the preliminary statement). [Source: Yiaga, 1 Mar 2023 and 26 Feb 2023]
* EU EOM: "widespread distribution of goods and vote buying"; on 18 March, vote buying by or for APC and PDP seen near polling units by five EU teams; credible reports of PVC buying. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]

### 3.6 Violence

* EU EOM: 101 campaign violence incidents with at least 74 deaths; seven killed in Enugu East two days before the 25 February poll, including the LP senatorial candidate; on 18 March, attacks on polling units in 21 states with about 21 deaths; 19 INEC officials abducted in Imo. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]
* Yiaga: intimidation, harassment or assault at 6 percent of sampled units on 25 February. [Source: Yiaga, 1 Mar 2023, fetched]

### 3.7 Cancelled results

* INEC did not publish turnout by state, accredited voters by party, or the list of cancelled or postponed units with reasons for the National Assembly elections. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched] Yiaga recorded no election in 13 of 1,507 sampled units (under 1 percent). [Source: Yiaga, 1 Mar 2023, fetched] CCIJ found 1,462 EC40G forms and 1,217 handwritten cancellation sheets on IReV. [Source: CCIJ repository, above] An official national count of cancelled polling units for the presidential election was not found. UNCERTAIN.
* Governorship elections were declared inconclusive in Adamawa and Kebbi. [Source: EU EOM final report, May 2023, fetched]

### 3.8 Court rulings on IReV

* Supreme Court, Atiku and Obi appeals, 26 October 2023, lead judgment by Justice John Okoro: IReV is not a collation centre but a portal for the public to view results; failure to transmit electronically cannot by itself invalidate an election (Section 135(1) of the 2022 Act); the IReV failure "may reduce" public confidence. [Source: https://www.channelstv.com/2023/10/26/irev-failure-may-reduce-voters-confidence-says-scourt/, 26 Oct 2023]
* Earlier, Oyetola v. INEC (2023) set the same tone; in Lawal v. Matawalle (Zamfara, 12 January 2024) the Supreme Court accepted IReV data to resolve disputed Maradun LGA results, treating IReV as a verification aid. [Source: PLAC Factsheet 2, Feb 2026, fetched]
* In court INEC argued its own guidelines could not override the Act and that public assurances by its officials had no evidential value, which PLAC calls a credibility gap. [Same source]

## 7. Pain points by process step

| # | Step | What fails | Evidence with numbers | Who is affected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Voter registration (CVR) | Register is inflated and never audited; deaths and migrations not removed; deduplication results not published | 2023: 2.8 million invalid registrations removed but no audit ever done, underage registrants of unknown number (EU EOM, May 2023). 2027: 10.6m to 10.77m new registrants, removals unpublished; Ekiti 2026 register differed by 71,109 between INEC and the returning officer (Dataphyte, 6 Jul 2026) | All voters; analysts, because turnout percentages are understated; parties disputing results |
| 2 | PVC printing and collection | Cards not collected; long queues and wrong centres; proxy collection and card buying; data released late and not by polling unit | 2023: 6,259,229 uncollected (6.7%), Lagos alone 845,225 (TheCable via Substack, 23 Feb 2023). Osun 2026: 432,843 uncollected (18.5%) (INEC PDF). Edo 2024: 379,245 uncollected (14.4%) (Dataphyte, 23 Sep 2024). Older backlog over 6 million (Tribune, 27 Dec 2024) | Voters who cannot vote without a card; urban and southern voters most; candidates in close races (margin of lead uses PVC counts) |
| 3 | Polling unit network | Very uneven unit sizes; voters moved or split without notice | 2023: units with fewer than 10 voters and others with over 10,000 (Yiaga, 1 Mar 2023); 240 units with zero voters (Ripples, 13 Feb 2023). FCT 2026: split units over 1,250 voters left voters unable to find units (INEC, 25 Feb 2026) | Voters at large units (queues, one BVAS); voters moved to new units |
| 4 | Logistics and materials | Materials and staff arrive late, so polls open late | 2023: officials at only 27% of units by 7:30 am; South East only 11% voting by 9:30 am (Yiaga, 1 Mar 2023); late opening in 44 of 50 EU-observed units, up to 5 hours late (EU EOM). FCT 2026: 45% open by 8:30 am (INEC). Osun 2026: 48% voting by 8:30 am (Yiaga, 16 Aug 2026); reprinting after a 22 July court order delayed some units | Voters (especially in the South East and South South), workers who cannot wait, turnout |
| 5 | Ad hoc staff and training | Staff cannot complete forms; missing staff; safety and pay concerns | 2023: only 31% of sampled presidential result forms on IReV formally and mathematically correct; 16 of 37 units had staff struggling with forms; staff missing in 18 of 50 units (EU EOM). 2027: 707,384 staff per day, about 90% corps members as presiding officers in many states (INEC, May and Oct 2026). 19 INEC officials abducted in Imo in March 2023 (EU EOM) | Corps members and their families; voters (errors, delays); parties (disputes from bad forms) |
| 6 | Accreditation (BVAS) | Device faults; fingerprint and face recognition fail, especially for older voters | 2023: BVAS malfunction at 10% of units (8% fixed, 2% replaced) (Yiaga, 1 Mar 2023). Osun 2026: malfunction at 20% of units; verified voters denied at 11% of units (Yiaga via AllAfrica, 17 Aug 2026) | Elderly voters, manual workers with worn fingerprints, voters at large single-BVAS units |
| 7 | Voting and counting | Vote buying; intimidation; secrecy breaches; result not posted | 2023: inducement at 6% of units, intimidation at 6% (Yiaga); 56 of 183 EU-observed units did not protect secrecy; results not posted in 24 of 37 units (EU EOM). Off-cycle: N1,000 to N5,000 per vote in Anambra 2025 (ICIR, 19 Nov 2025); N5,000 to N20,000 in Ondo 2024 (Gazette, 16 Nov 2024); N10,000 to N20,000 and over 30 incidents in Ekiti 2026 (Dataphyte, 6 Jul 2026) | Poor voters; honest candidates; overall legitimacy |
| 8 | Result upload to IReV | Software failure in 2023; illegible, wrong or missing images; uploads from outside polling units; law still lets paper override when transmission fails | 2023: first presidential sheet 8:55 pm; 20% by noon next day; over 25% missing at declaration (INEC report via Channels, 23 Feb 2024; EU EOM). 7,493 never uploaded, 12,054 blurred, 2,294 wrong election (CCIJ data). Better since: Anambra 99.39% in about 4 hours (8 Nov 2025); Osun 98.35% by next morning but a 3 hour stall with 471 outstanding (16 to 17 Aug 2026) | Public, media and observers who track results; parties and courts that need evidence |
| 9 | Collation (ward, LGA, state, national) | Chaotic centres; ward level skipped; results changed between polling unit and collation; IReV not used to check | 2023: many ward centres did not open in the South South and South East; discrepancies in 13 of 41 observed centres on 18 March (EU EOM). Edo 2024: Yiaga and Situation Room said results were altered at collation in five LGAs; 29,976 unexplained gap between accredited and votes cast (AllAfrica, 25 Sep 2024; Guardian, 25 Sep 2024). Ekiti 2026: 452 of about 2,400 sheets flagged, over 100 altered (Dataphyte) | Voters whose choice is changed; losing parties; public trust |
| 10 | Declaration | Key data not published; margin of lead and cancellations opaque | 2023: INEC did not publish turnout by state or the list of cancelled units with reasons for National Assembly polls (EU EOM). Rivers and Imo presidential results outside Yiaga's statistical range (Yiaga, 1 Mar 2023). Adamawa and Kebbi governorship inconclusive (EU EOM) | Parties, observers and analysts; voters in cancelled units |
| 11 | Petitions and tribunals | Very high litigation volume; IReV failures not a legal ground; long timelines | 2023: 1,196 petitions; 891 (74.5%) dismissed or withdrawn (INEC via Blueprint, 20 Oct 2023). Supreme Court held IReV is not a collation centre and non-transmission cannot void an election (Channels, 26 Oct 2023). 180 day trial window unchanged; 60 day bill still pending (Daily Trust, 18 Sep 2026) | Petitioners (cost and delay); winners whose mandate is in doubt; public trust |
| 12 | Cross-cutting: turnout and trust | Participation keeps falling | Presidential turnout 69.08% in 2003, 34.75% in 2019, 26.72% in 2023 (EU EOM). Ondo 2024: 25%, lowest since 1999 (Dataphyte). Anambra 2025: 21.4%. FCT 2026: about 15% (INEC) | Everyone; especially the young, who were 76.6% of new 2023 registrants (EU EOM) but are now only 28.79% of the register (INEC, Oct 2026) |
